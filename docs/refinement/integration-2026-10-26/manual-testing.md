1. Run the real camera with `?perf` for 60 seconds and again at ten minutes. Apply the existing **at least 10 analyzed fps** preflight. If it fails, stop the demonstration on that device and record the failure. The Windows headless 4–5.25 fps result does not approve the physical laptop. A true sensor-to-screen claim requires independent timing of physical motion and display, not just these internal clocks.
2. Check left/right and mirrored overlay alignment at the frame edges and after resizing/orientation changes. Test both natural hand dominance and hands leaving/re-entering view. Verify pause/resume, tab hide/show, deny-then-allow permission, camera unplug/reconnect where supported, rapid start/cancel and recovery without duplicate workers or stale overlays. Log memory at one and ten minutes, including limits of browser heap metrics.
3. Follow MAN1: six ASL words twice, recording correct/wrong/missed/false outcomes and allowing the protocol's four seconds after hands lower. Add natural slow/fast signing, pauses, small/large comfortable movements, immediate repeats and separated repeats. Keep repeats and transitions in the record rather than discarding difficult attempts. Confirm speech happens only for confirmed transcript entries and stops when requested (MAN5).
4. Follow MAN2's 30-second idle and ordinary-gesture controls. Include incomplete THANK YOU strokes and SORRY circles, clothing/glasses adjustment, conversation gestures and camera movement. Count confident false accepts; abstention is preferable to an unearned accepted word. Follow MAN3 offline rehearsal and MAN4 permissions/recovery.
5. Check static signs, language switching during load and while active, transcript isolation, personal-sign recording/save/reload/removal and legacy examples. Non-ASL target evaluation needs a fluent reviewer and the actual shipped language-specific class; do not substitute ASL gestures or synonyms for absent labels.
