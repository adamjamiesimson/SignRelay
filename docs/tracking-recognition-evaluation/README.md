# Tracking lanes and dynamic-sign evaluation

This branch starts at [PR #58](https://github.com/adamjamiesimson/SignRelay/pull/58), `6fad3a4a1fa80e24d6caafbead4668ce2f3fb323`. The previous [sprint](../ultimate-sprint/README.md) fixed faults but left visible fast-motion lag and signer accuracy unresolved.

## Tracking hypothesis and comparison

The current vision worker posts hand landmarks before it runs face and pose. `LiveVision` still keeps the frame slot occupied until the combined result arrives. Early paint helps the current frame. It cannot give the next frame access to the same worker during auxiliary work. [Earlier licensed RGB profiling](https://github.com/adamjamiesimson/SignRelay/actions/runs/41595982233) measured 51.4 ms median for hands and 91.3 ms p95 for combined CPU work. Alternating face and pose reduced total p95 to 75.7 ms, but changed 3/346 starter outputs, made cues older and barely changed throughput. That schedule remains rejected for public use.

The new diagnostic compares the **exact checked-in PR #58 vision worker** with separate hand and auxiliary workers. Both use the pinned MediaPipe tasks and the same two-hand gesture options. Each lane holds at most one in-flight frame and the scheduler retains one latest source index. Auxiliary results are reused only when their timestamp is at or before the hand frame and at most 200 ms old. A cache miss delivers empty auxiliary arrays. No hand coordinates are interpolated or synthesized. Split work can contend for CPU, allocate another WASM runtime and require an additional bitmap. The experiment is built only by the diagnostic script and never into public exports.

The two variants use the same display-timed replay scheduler, which offers decoded PNGs at source timestamps and starts the latest observation once the lane is free. This isolates the effect of waiting for auxiliary inference. It is not a byte-for-byte recreation of the app's RAF scheduler or camera decode. Runs alternate by clip and round on the same hosted CPU. The workflow verifies the existing exposed FSboard cohort's bytes with its recorded digests and keeps RGB, landmarks and clip identifiers on the runner. Output includes startup, delivered and dropped frames, hand compute, source-arrival-to-draw, next RAF opportunity, result delivery, inter-overlay gaps, gaps over 200 ms, cue age, hand-count disagreement at shared source frames, and tracking loss and recovery transitions. The report also records any unsupported starter candidate and its timing relative to the annotated clip end. RAF is a presentation opportunity. Neither sensor latency nor physical screen presentation is measured. Hand-count transitions and inter-overlay gaps are proxies for tracking loss and freezes, and the clips have no per-frame hand ground truth. FSboard fingerspelling supports a real-hand performance comparison and unsupported-input check. It cannot estimate starter recall, and its two-hand coverage is limited.

A dense PR #58 reference pass marks common fast-motion slots before either real-time run. A slot is rapid when a uniquely labelled wrist moves at least 0.7 normalized image units per second over at most 100 ms during the annotated interval. This provides the same denominator for fast-slot delivery and draw latency. It is conditioned on successful tracking by the reference and can miss motion during occlusion or reference failure.

## Recognition hypotheses

The exposed WLASL starter study found that similar movements can arrive with different articulation or completion. THANK YOU can share an outward path with BAD and SAD. SORRY and PLEASE share chest-level trajectories with confusables such as PLAY and HAPPY. HELLO competes with other forehead movements. YES needs a small wrist nod and was missed at natural amplitudes. NO needs discriminating index, middle and thumb closure. The underlying confusions and sample support are in the [starter reliability report](../release-candidate/STARTER-RELIABILITY.md). Altering a threshold on this cohort cannot establish independent accuracy.

The research harness now compares four models on identical signer-disjoint folds of the **exposed** WLASL captures. It prints per-sign precision, recall and abstention, and false accepts on other signs. The original starter approximation and frozen DTW model remain comparators. `articulated-timed` uses 3D joint alignment rather than wrist-to-tip reach for finger extension, resamples by elapsed time, refuses to fill a dominant-hand dropout with the opposite hand, and segments only the longest observation span bounded by 240 ms gaps. The joint channels are a compact articulation descriptor. They do not establish signer identity, and normalized MediaPipe axes can still vary with aspect ratio. `articulated-guarded` additionally calibrates distance and rival margin against other signs and the next target, using training signers only. It abstains if no threshold pair with a correct calibration hit and the required negative rejection can be found. The original baseline can fall back to an infinite absolute threshold or choose its smallest tested margin even if every tested margin misses its false-accept target.

The variants change several feature choices together, so an improvement would motivate a separate articulation, timing and hand-continuity ablation. The guarded variant isolates the extra rejection policy. Neither variant runs in the app. The clips are already exposed, and folds exclude signers from each other while providing no untouched dataset. Clip-based DTW waits for the clip and does not measure live completion. Before adoption, collect consented, independently labelled signers using the existing study protocol. Freeze the hypotheses and evaluate held-out people performing these signs, nearby confusables, everyday movement and unsupported signs. Annotate actual sign boundaries and include small YES nods, repeat signs, intentional pauses, left- and right-dominant signers, and two-hand motion and occlusion.

## Reproduce

Local controls on Windows passed 20 focused tests and TypeScript checking. On one 73-frame **no-hand synthetic** real-WASM replay pair, combined/split delivered 28/41 frames and source-to-overlay p95 was 131/116 ms. This checks the scheduler under load; order and one pair preclude a performance claim. On 60 synthetic target and 55 synthetic negative sequences, starter rules recalled 60 and falsely accepted 20. Frozen DTW recalled 53, articulated-timed 56, and articulated-guarded 58, with zero accepts on those synthetic negatives. These counts only verify mechanics and do not justify deployment. Hosted licensed comparisons are tracked in the draft PR.

- [Licensed RGB workflow](../../.github/workflows/vision-lanes-rgb.yml) runs `tracking-determinism-prepare.py` then `node scripts/diagnostics/run-vision-lanes.mjs CHROME outputs/vision-lanes.json`. Install the pinned vision assets first. The command expects verified runner-local decoded inputs under `work/determinism/`.
- [WLASL starter workflow](../../.github/workflows/starter-reliability.yml) captures through the pinned vision runtime and invokes `node scripts/research/temporal-experiment.mjs wlasl CLIPS_JSON CAPTURES_DIR REPORT_JSON`. `synthetic` mode checks mechanics only. Use a separate, consented cohort for any untouched evaluation.

## Physical device check for the owner

1. Use the two protected builds supplied for comparison on the same phone or laptop, in the same browser and room. Close extra tabs and keep the camera, lighting and distance fixed. Run them in A/B/B/A order, three times per device if possible. Use the local workspace with `?perf` appended to the address to see the tracker, overlay and recognition timing readout. Screenshots of timing readouts should exclude the camera view.
2. Start the camera and let it settle for 15 seconds. Repeat a slow sweep, a fast sweep, an abrupt reversal, and a two-hand crossing. Then briefly cover one hand and uncover it. Note which build freezes, visibly trails, loses a hand, or recovers late. Record browser, CPU/GPU shown in the readout, resolution if known, and capture-to-overlay p50/p95. Compare the same device and delegate.
3. With a consenting ASL signer, perform the six target signs separately, then run confusables and everyday actions. Mark the moment each sign finishes. Record the first candidate, whether it is right, wrong or absent, and the delay from completion. Keep mistaken suggestions separate from words deliberately added. Share only aggregate timing and counts unless participants explicitly consent to a different process. This is a supervised dry run, separate from the sealed evaluation cohort.

Keep public production on PR #58's scheduling and suggestion policy pending results. The split worker and temporal variants are diagnostic experiments. They do not establish a public release case. No production deployment is needed for either experiment.
